Couples, dating and the shared report

What exists, what does not, and the constraints that matter.

Built

More exists than expected

Partner linking, invite codes and consent are modelled, including a both-partners flag. Four real modules sit behind them: the relationship matrix, cherish and honor, love language, and intimacy. Thirty couple prompts and thirty dating prompts are written.

Build

The shared report does not exist at all

Nothing takes what one partner entered and shows the other anything. This is the half that makes the couple tier worth $47. Proposal: a weekly digest per opted-in module, delivered to each partner separately, so neither sees the other's raw answers.

Two constraints that are the product, not the polish.

Consent per module, never blanket. The intimacy module holds the most sensitive data anywhere in the ecosystem. Linking a partner must not silently authorize sharing it. Either partner can withdraw one module without unlinking.

Appreciations and requests only. Your words were "what they enjoy, like, or would like more of, in a loving and proper way." That rules out scores about a partner, comparisons between them, and anything framed as a deficit. "She would love more of this" ships. "You are falling short here" does not, and that is what most relationship software builds by accident.

Build

Two individuals merging into one couple account

Separate logins, one shared dashboard, one subscription. It turns Faith Match into the feeder for the couple tier and keeps both people instead of losing them when they stop being single. Three decisions first: whose card continues and whether the other is prorated, what happens to two founding locks when only one is needed and whether the freed seat returns to the hundred, and how a separation unwinds.

The unwind is not an edge case in a relationship product. Splitting has to be a built feature: the shared dashboard dissolves, each person keeps only their own data, and billing returns to two subscriptions. Built after the fact, it gets built badly, during someone's worst week.
